Architecture Decision: Unified Media Timeline with PyAV

Navigation
  • Other language version
  • Core EN manual
  • Previous decision: direct libndi sender

After moving the sender to direct libndi, long-run drift still appeared. That shifted the focus: the problem no longer looked like only a wrapper issue, but also a consequence of how the streamer reconstructed audio and video time.

1. Problem

The previous model decoupled audio and video:

  • video paced by fps,
  • audio fully decoded and sliced according to the video frame index.

That approach introduced two implicit clocks and made accumulated drift plausible in long sessions, especially with fractional fps or source changes.

2. Decision

The new architecture uses PyAV as a unified container reader. Every audio or video event carries media time, and the runtime uses a single playback clock.

3. Applied changes

  • media_reader.py introduces the single timeline.
  • stream_video.py now consumes MediaVideoEvent and MediaAudioEvent.
  • utils.py creates the sender with NDI clocks disabled to avoid a second timing authority.
  • requirements.txt pins av==17.1.0.

4. What we gain

  • less manual error-prone timing logic,
  • better behavior at 23.976/24/59.94/60 fps,
  • less coupling between video cadence and audio delivery,
  • a much clearer architecture for the team.

5. Tradeoffs

  • a new dependency enters the runtime: PyAV,
  • its installation must be validated on target machines,
  • ffmpeg.py remains as a legacy helper and can be cleaned later.

6. Current validation status

  1. NDI Monitor,
  2. Unity Editor,
  3. Unity AVP Simulator,
  4. Apple Vision Pro device build.

Observed result: the streamer now behaves correctly on these targets after the move to a unified PyAV media timeline.

7. Recommended QA from this point

  1. keep long-session soak runs as an operational regression test,
  2. continue comparing NDI Monitor and Unity whenever a new or suspicious source appears,
  3. repeat validation with 24/23.976 and 60/59.94 fps sources,
  4. keep hot source changes and sender restarts as standard regression cases.

8. Summary

PyAV is not being introduced to make the project heavier. It is being introduced to remove the most suspicious piece of the previous model: reconstructing audio from the temporal grid of video instead of respecting the real timeline carried by the media container.
